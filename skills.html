02

Embedded

Firmware written to exercise the hardware rather than to ship a product: bring-up, functional test, and the measurements that decide whether a design claim holds. Enough to find the fault, and enough to know when the fault is mine.

STM32 F4, G4, H5ESP32-C3 and S3nRF52832 CC++Python STM32CubeMXESP-IDFPlatformIO CMakeRTOS I²CSPIUARTBLEMQTT

Enabling the GPIO wake feature alongside an edge interrupt left the patient button drawing 18 mA instead of 0.4 mA. Removing it brought average current from 0.90 to 0.60 mA and battery life from 46 to 69 days.

03

Measurement and verification

The part I care about most. Checking how a number was produced before accepting what it says: whether the method suited the question, whether the reading was taken correctly, and whether the processing was right. That applies to my own results before anyone else's, and to data other people hand me.

OscilloscopeLogic analyserSpectrometer MultimeterCurrent measurement Sensor calibration against reference instruments Test plans traced to requirements Incoming acceptance criteria Reviewing methods and data produced by others Root-cause analysis

A proposed EMC fix measured 23.4 dB better, but at rest, in the condition that had already passed. In the one that had actually failed it changed nothing, so it was rejected. The product passed retest with 9.35 dB of margin.

Standards I design against

Not a reading list. Each of these has changed something in a board I drew, or decided whether a product could be released.

IEC 60601-1
Basic safety for medical equipment. Isolation, creepage, touch current.
IEC 60601-1-2
Electromagnetic compatibility. The clause behind the conducted emission retest.
IEC 60601-2-19
Baby incubators. Independent thermal cut-out, alarm thresholds, reconnection.
CISPR 11
Emission limits, Class A Group 1 for the operating table.
ISO 14971
Risk management. Hazard, cause, protection, verification, gap.
SNI equivalents
The Indonesian adoptions that national regulatory testing is run against.
